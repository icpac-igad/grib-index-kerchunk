  Stakeholder framing (the "win-win-win" without re-stating tex)

  The cost model in 07 outputs three POVs:

  - Provider (NOAA/ECMWF) — Δ cost = 0 (they already store GRIB + sidecar). Adds
   zero burden, hence "win 1".
  - ARCO publisher / regional centre — Δ cost = storage-of-duplicate −
  GIK-parquet-host (~$80K/yr saved at AWS list, today's catalog). "Win 2".
  - End-user analyst — Δ cost = full-download egress + decode − GIK byte-range
  egress + decode. Quantify per analysis-slice and per analyst-year. "Win 3"
  (per analysis is cheaper) but also surfaces the break-even where it isn't
  (heavy daily power-user with 10+ full-archive scans → narrows).

  That break-even is the nuance the user wants — concretely: at how many
  full-archive materialisations per year does GIK consumer-side compute exceed
  the duplicated-storage cost? 07_cost_model.py outputs the curve.

  What we won't duplicate from the tex

  - Validation correlation (already done in gefs/validate_gik_vs_herbie_* and
  ecmwf/validate_gik_vs_herbie_2025.py) — cost-eval/ will import & cite those,
  not re-run.
  - Pipeline architecture description.
  - Performance table for the 12-var × 51-member × 9-step ECMWF slice — 05 will
  re-measure it for traceability but the framing is cost, not benchmarking.

  The honest reflection — when does each method win?

  1. Global / many-variable / many-init dump. GIK is roughly parity with Zarr in
   cost (Zarr 3-8× smaller on wire from compression alone) — but GIK can also be
   re-built incrementally from public .idx files in minutes per date. GIK wins 
  on flexibility + zero ARCO duplication cost.
  2. Regional analysis (East Africa, Sahel, etc.). Zarr is 100-500× more 
  efficient on the wire because it can subset spatially via chunks. GIK/Herbie
  must pull every full global message and crop locally. Zarr decisively wins on 
  bytes if the chunking matches the access pattern (dynamical's ECMWF chunking
  is essentially perfect for this).
  3. Pencil / station-time-series queries. Zarr wins by 3-4 orders of magnitude
  because GIK's per-message floor (~2-5 MB) is paid for every (var, mem, step,
  init) tuple even if you want one pixel. This is the workload where the GIK
  architecture is structurally wrong.
  4. The product-coverage caveat. Dynamical's ECMWF Zarr is 19 curated vars ×
  00z only; their GEFS is the 35-day extension (181 leads). GIK indexes the full
   GRIB archive (~100+ vars × all 4 runs × the operational forecast horizon).
  GIK gives you variable coverage Zarr doesn't. A regional centre asking "should
   we duplicate or use GIK" must scope: if their work is in the
  dynamical-curated subset, Zarr is operationally better; outside it, GIK is the
   only option that doesn't require building+hosting an ARCO catalog.
  5. The honest "win-win-win" updated. It's not "GIK beats Zarr". It's:
    - GIK lets a regional centre avoid the $52 K/yr dynamical-style hosting bill
   entirely while keeping full archive variable coverage
    - Trade-off: regional users pay ~$1-2 per regional slice in egress on GIK if
   cross-cloud, vs $0.004 on dynamical Zarr — annualised this is small (~$700/yr
   at 1 slice/day) but real
    - The structural limitation Zarr fixes (spatial subsetting) is genuine and
  the GIK story shouldn't claim otherwise. The two methods are complementary:
  GIK for broad coverage + ad-hoc access, Zarr for high-frequency regional /
  pencil workloads
